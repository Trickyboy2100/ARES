\begin{abstract}
Multi-stage contact manipulation requires more than reaching intermediate positions: object orientation, motion and tool contact persist into subsequent stages. We study constrained residual reinforcement learning on nominal robot control, using task-relative geometry and past execution effects as conditioning signals. A JAKA testbed executes two-stage free-object pushing (P1) and guided-slot insertion (P2) in 16 independent PhysX environments. Historical three-seed evaluation yields 13, 18 and 23 successes out of 32 unseen-geometry cases for ordinary residual PPO versus 8 for damped feedback, but P2 alone yields only 6, 6 and 7 out of 16 versus 5. A new matched-budget P2 development pilot compares BODY-XY residuals, task-frame residuals and effect-history conditioning using Isaac Sim 5.1 and Isaac Lab 2.3.0. With 16,384 interactions per main model, their independently accepted counts are 30/29/30, 30/30/30 and 30/30/30 out of 32; corrected task-local feedback also achieves 30/32. Input interventions change applied references and object trajectories but preserve successful-case sets. The outcome-aware increment therefore fails its preregistered promotion criterion. The new cohort accounts for 196,608 online interactions including retained software diagnostics. These results establish executable training and a useful engineering baseline, not a validated novel algorithmic advantage. Further progress requires genuinely actionable contact parameters and independent mechanism evidence. No real-robot, visual-policy or hardware-safety claim is made.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} contact manipulation; residual reinforcement learning; skill parameters; task conditioning; reproducible robotics.
