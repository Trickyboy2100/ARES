# One paper, one falsifiable claim

Working title: Task-Conditioned Skill-Boundary Parameter Reinforcement Learning for Reliable Multi-Stage Manipulation.

Question: when several physically legal predecessor terminal states exist, can a policy choose an executable parameter before release and then adapt a second skill after actually observing released, stable objects, with a better rescue/regression tradeoff than matched ordinary RL and strong non-RL choices?

The current implementation is a privileged-state Panda/StackPyramid-v1 study. ARES-R is industrial motivation only: no hardware, point-cloud perception, JAKA transfer, or production integration is evaluated. Parameterization, downstream value, and subtracting nominal value are prior ideas, not novelty claims. The tested candidate is ordinary two-boundary PPO with a training-only paired downside-risk regularizer. Its independent benefit is a hypothesis; negative results narrow the paper instead of being hidden.

A publication claim requires: two independently useful physical parameters; verified intermediate release/stability; independent scenes and multiple learning seeds; gains beyond the identical-interface PPO and supervised/search baselines; a mechanism ablation; then sealed tests, a distinct task and perception robustness. This pilot does not imply these conditions are satisfied.
