# Primary-source comparison

- [AWAC](https://awacrl.github.io/), [official rlkit implementation](https://github.com/rail-berkeley/rlkit), inspected commit `ac45a9db24b89d97369bef302487273bcc3e3d84`, `rlkit/torch/sac/awac_trainer.py:555–645`: learned Q minus policy value supplies detached exponential/normalized likelihood weights; implementation also supports reparameterized Actor gradients. Advantage-weighted imitation and offline-to-online reuse are established. Our measured finite physical comparison is not a calibrated learned advantage, and adding it to SAC does not by itself establish originality.
- [Cal-QL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05479), [official source](https://github.com/nakamotoo/Cal-QL), inspected commit `ac6eafec22e8d60836573e1f488c7f626ce8a77e`, `JaxCQL/conservative_sac.py:180–250`: MC return lower bounds calibrate the conservative loss on sampled policy actions; this is different from assigning a binary success difference to current entropy-regularized Q. We do not copy that claim or treat old Q as success probability. No external package is installed or executed.
- Policy Decorator's controlled exploration, RecoveryChaining's return to nominal control, STAP's downstream feasibility and ViSkill's successor-value transfer remain inherited precedents. Their pinned source inspections and limitations are retained in the prior action_frontier/joint_continuation releases. No official leaderboard reproduction is asserted here.

The present candidate tests **which physical suffix a preference label describes and when to retire that label**. Reference snapshots, empirical KL and paired ranking are mature components. An independent contribution requires a complete-task benefit beyond same-data SAC, supervised ranking and a matched retention/refresh ablation. Without those controls, improved scores support a candidate only, not novelty or safety.

License check: rlkit has MIT LICENSE. The inspected Cal-QL checkout has no top-level LICENSE; it was read only, with no code copied into the learner or executed.

## Executed-witness revision, after the first actual online results

- [GEM](https://github.com/facebookresearch/GradientEpisodicMemory), inspected commit `34c6b8e9a0607db7567301c48b727430d20bee7e`, `model/gem.py`: episodic-memory gradient constraints and dual-QP projection are established. The source is CC BY-NC 4.0; inspected only, no code copied or run. The new prototype independently implements sequential logit-margin halfspace corrections of an Adam displacement, followed by an exact finite-state action-membership check and backtracking. Projection itself is not a contribution.
- [SPIBB](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/laroche19a/laroche19a.pdf), [source](https://github.com/RomainLaroche/SPIBB), inspected commit `833f32b642759111907d2c4d086d1ae1ce9d84cb`, `spibb.py`: bootstrapping uncertain state-actions to a baseline is established. Our empirical witness set has neither its count-based MDP assumptions nor a high-probability safety guarantee. In particular, a fallback action is distinguished from an actually successful alternative, and the Actor still has only 83 observable features.

The bounded next comparison is **soft set-valued witness imitation versus projected Actor updates on exactly the same measured witness set**, both with the same SAC/BC/ranking and actual own-policy refresh. Successful full trajectories supply prefix and suffix decisions; measured positive or both-success branches expand permissible support. Explicit failed-baseline fallback keeps the initial policy feasible but is never labelled successful. No deployment-time lookup overrides the network. Training exploration is unchanged and is not guaranteed safe. The goal is to test whether finite action retention changes full native behavior, not to establish a new general projection theorem.

Further primary-source checks during training:

- [Rerouted Behavior Improvement, IJCAI2020](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2020/396), Sections4.4–4.5 and6: behavior-relative probability constraints and nonparametric action-ranking improvement already address unreliable Q-driven policy changes. A generic claim that behavior constraints prevent degradation would therefore be insufficient. Our finite executed-choice constraints operate on network updates and measured branch witnesses; this distinction still needs empirical value, not just different notation.
- [Frank-Wolfe action-constrained policy optimization, UAI2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11055): the abstract explicitly identifies projection-related zero-gradient problems and separates action feasibility from parameter updates. Its continuous physical action constraints differ from the present finite neural greedy-choice constraints. Our stalled solver is a numerical observation, not discovery of the general projection-stalling problem.
- [PIRL/PIPO, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00860v5): the checked abstract describes measuring cross-iteration improvement and modulating learning against a historical performance anchor, primarily for language/agent post-training. We inspected the abstract, not reproduced its code. Merely calling this work a closed-loop policy-improvement framework is not an originality claim.

SPIBB source license is MIT. GEM, SPIBB, AWAC and Cal-QL source reads remain separate from our independently written implementation; no restricted-license source was copied.
