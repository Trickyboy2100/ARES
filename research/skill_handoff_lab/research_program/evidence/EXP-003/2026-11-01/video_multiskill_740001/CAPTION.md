**INTERPRETATION.** Different executable grasp choices change the measured attachment and remaining budget, and strong geometric successor replanning reacts with different hook parameters. The video documents two genuine parameter decisions and an intervening physical SkillResult, rather than relabeling two waypoints as skills. Red is the actual native tool; blue is the actual native cube. The failure branch pushes the cube farther away while the strong-baseline branch pulls it closer. The camera is fixed and was widened to retain both tool and cube through the complete trajectory. Projection of all native cube corners and the conservative native tool bounds confirms they remain inside the800×600 pixels of each branch throughout.

**NOT ESTABLISHED.** This is a deterministic strong-baseline win against a legal parameter intervention, not a new RL method, trained-policy gain, independent success-rate estimate or proof that grasp alone explains the difference. Grasp, attachment, successor parameter choice and available steps differ jointly. This case is not an ablation that isolates each factor. Source trace observations are simulation physical feedback, not a demonstrated real-world sensing pipeline. No real robot moves and no restricted field data are used.

Both RAW and ANNOTATED are **MEASURED_POSE_REPLAY** reconstructed from all recorded joint and object poses, with zero added physics steps. They are not images originally captured during online dynamics. Unneeded velocities are not reconstructed for graphics; these replay states must not be reused as physics initializations. TRACE is **DATA_DERIVED_PLOT**. Framek represents physical stepk at k/20seconds, with initial frame0 and terminal frame100. Both videos contain101 frames and encode5.05seconds. Decision/SkillResult cards appear only after their recorded boundary, although terminal outcome is explicitly shown as offline analysis throughout.
