There is no demonstrated conditional reversal of the preferred axis, and no failure remaining for learning to rescue in this baseline panel. The two successful baseline endpoints in each scene likewise do not establish useful first-action conditional ranking. This bounded panel supports continuing strong-controller analysis rather than training RL on avoidable pregrasp-only planning mistakes.

They are budget/tracking-limited suffix executions, not precursor failures. Shared serial arm scheduling remains a known control limitation; do not turn it into an RL claim.

## Nominal value signal: ranking versus absolute feasibility

The model includes complete pregrasp/descent/lift/goal geometry, six close steps and six static-reserve steps. Actual execution uses measured attachment and goal feedback. Prediction is not a calibrated probability or a learned critic.

- Complete model correctly prefers the successful axis in all four paired first-skill states; two alternatives also succeed, two fail.
- On ten episodes reaching native success, predicted completion exceeds time to first native success by2–7steps (mean4.1, median4.0). First success and final success are recorded separately; this panel has no observed final degradation after first success.
- Treating predicted duration≤remaining budget as a binary success contract is overly conservative: TP3, FP0, FN7, TN2. The controller deliberately does not reject every predicted-budget violation; seven such executions succeed physically.
- Failed episode completion time is right-censored at native100; no fabricated completion duration is substituted.

Therefore a coarse budget gate is not an adequate value label, but its ranking is already sufficient on this panel. Merely learning to correct the six-step reserve would be scheduling/model calibration, not automatically an independent skill-level RL contribution. Additional RL is unjustified until new physical conditions produce reproducible residual choices that a strong successor model cannot simply resolve.

The native cube has symmetric geometry and the current parameter axes may permit a simple consistent geometric winner. This is an evidence-based limitation, not proof that all two-robot skill adaptation is impossible. Next investigations should distinguish contact/physics mismatch from redundant axis representation and concurrent scheduling before introducing a learning claim.

No new seeds, training or extra endpoint.05 alternatives were added because the prerequisite conditional-opportunity signal was absent. All original weak-controller failures remain in the earlier multiskill_two_robot archive and are not mixed into this stronger baseline's8/8 figure.
